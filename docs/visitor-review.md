# Visitor walkthrough

This is a guide for an observed session, not a claim that a visitor study is complete.
Use two people and disposable local rooms. Keep private links, seat tokens, recovery
codes, and personal notes out of recordings or public reports.

1. Ask one person to make a room. Observe whether they understand the companion role
   and whom the link is for, without explaining the two seats first.
2. Ask them to name her, choose a look, and leave notes. Watch focus, long text,
   save/cancel, and whether changing appearance feels distinct from writing notes.
3. Let the second person open the invitation. Check that they understand they become
   the cat and that opening a link does not join before they choose to go in.
4. Let both explore. Ask the companion to find an action, and the cat to leave a reply
   or drawing. Notice whether the scene's quiet details are discoverable through
   keyboard controls as well as touch or pointer interaction.
5. Ask them to send an invitation and make a postcard. Check whether saving and sharing
   the prepared image are clear and whether cancelling a share sheet feels safe.
6. Ask them to find their recovery options and explain how to return on another browser.
   Use disposable data for any recovery, seat-release, leave, or delete exercise.

Repeat a writing task during an intercepted failed request. The draft must stay intact
with clear feedback and a retry. Try offline use, blocked clipboard access, storage refusal,
reduced motion, keyboard navigation, enlarged text, and both room palettes.

Record the revision, date, device/browser, completed task, hesitation, unexpected outcome,
and recovery. Separate what the person discovered from explanations you gave them.
Prioritize lost work, a blocked task, or misunderstood seat/privacy rules ahead of cosmetic
preferences. See [quality checks](quality.md) for automated coverage and its limits.
